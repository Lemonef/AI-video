# Cinematic guide: design, sound, sync

The rules every episode is built against. Researched 2026-10-27; sources at the end. Most are practitioner guidance rather than settled research, so treat numbers as starting points and check the result on a phone speaker.

## 0. Priority: emotion first

When rules conflict, give up the lower ones first (Walter Murch's Rule of Six): **emotion** (51%) > story (23%) > rhythm > eye trace > 2D screen plane > 3D space. Every cut, move and sound must serve the feeling of its beat.

## 1. Picture

1. **Hook first.** The first frame states the premise visually and reads with the sound off. Open on the most interesting line, and don't answer the hook's question early.
2. **Shot progression builds dread.** Start wide: negative space reads as vulnerability. As tension rises, a slow push-in toward the face, subtle enough that it goes unnoticed. A close-up lands on the realisation. A locked, still frame means "something could enter".
3. **Motivated movement only.** The camera moves because the story does. No constant drift or shake; a jolt only on a scare beat.
4. **Cut on rhythm, roughly every 3–5 seconds** for short-form, and never in the middle of a word.
5. **Colour script.** The palette follows the emotional arc (safe → uneasy → danger), not a single grade for the whole episode.
6. **One subject per frame.** UI stays small and quiet.

## 2. Sound

7. **Establish the normal sound first** (the crickets) so that its change is felt.
8. **Silence is a weapon.** Drop to near-silence just before the reveal, then hit. Never fill a quiet moment with filler.
9. **Score emotion, not movement** (no "Mickey Mousing"). Cuts are silent; one-shots only on story beats, and never the same sound on every line.
10. **Off-screen sound beats a visible threat:** hear it before you see it.
11. **One simple repeated motif builds dread** (the heartbeat), escalating over the episode.
12. **Mix:** ambience at least about 15 LU under the voice, music at least about 10 LU under. Duck 10–20 dB under speech, check on phone speakers, and master around −14 LUFS (a YouTube reference; TikTok publishes no figure).

## 3. Sync

13. **J-cut:** the next scene's sound starts before its picture (anticipation). **L-cut:** a sound carries over the cut (continuity). Hard sound cuts only for shocks.
14. **Hits land on words**, via `word` cues on the Whisper timings, not on a guessed fraction of the line.
15. **Captions** appear as the words are spoken, short enough to read (about 12–15 characters per second for Latin script; Thai reads more slowly, so keep chunks short), with a gap of about 0.125s between them, and never starting or ending exactly on a cut.
